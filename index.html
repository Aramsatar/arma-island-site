The face

Glance up. You already know.

She reacts to the load, not just to whether something is running. One agent looks different from four — and an agent that needs an answer looks different from both. Every state is an animation, not a status dot.

Call her with ⌥⌘I. She opens as a square under the notch and tells you what's running — out loud, in a voice already on your Mac. No microphone, no permission to grant, and still nothing sent anywhere. Press it again and she's gone.

Don't like her? Drop your own artwork into a folder and it replaces her, one mood at a time — PNG, SVG or video. The folder is watched, so it changes while you're looking at it.

The loop

Four things, without leaving what you're doing.

An agent that needs you and can't reach you is just a stopped agent.

01

Watch

Every session at a glance — the prompt you gave it, the file it's editing right now, its task list, and how long it's been going.

02

Answer

When Claude Code asks permission, the island opens itself and shows you what it wants to run. Allow or Deny right there. It stays open until you decide.

03

Jump

Click a session and land in the exact window and tab it's running in — iTerm, Terminal, Ghostty, Cursor, VS Code.

04

Hear

A sound when a run finishes. Pick it, or turn it off. You'll know a build ended from the other side of the room.

Privacy

There is nothing to breach.

Not a policy — a property of how it's built. There is no networking code in the app at all. It makes no requests and holds no sockets while running. There is no server for it to talk to.

Why

Built by someone who kept losing the terminal.

Agents changed the shape of a working day. You start something, go and think about something else, and come back to find it finished twenty minutes ago — or worse, stopped four minutes in because it wanted permission and nobody was watching.

The notch is the one part of the screen you always look at and never use. It's above every window, it's already black, and it's in your eyeline. That's the whole idea.

ARMA Island is a small, deliberate tool. It is not a platform. It has no account, no cloud, and no plans to grow into one. You buy it once and it keeps working.
