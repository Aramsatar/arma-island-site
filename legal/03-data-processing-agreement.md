# Data Processing Agreement — ARMA Island

**Status: you do not need this yet. Keep it ready anyway.**

**Last updated:** [DATE]

## Why this document opens with a disclaimer

A DPA governs the case where *we* process personal data **on behalf of your
organisation**. For ARMA Island as it stands today, that case does not exist:

- The app has no network permission and no server. Data it reads — your
  clipboard, your agent sessions, your now-playing — **never reaches us**. We
  are not processing it on anyone's behalf; we never receive it.
- The only personal data we hold is the **name and email of the person who
  bought a licence**, and for that we are the *controller*, not a processor. It
  is covered by the Privacy Policy, not by this agreement.

Handing a customer a DPA that describes processing you do not do is worse than
having none: it creates obligations you cannot audit against and promises a
relationship that does not exist.

**So why does this file exist?** Because enterprise buyers ask for a DPA as a
procurement reflex, and "we don't need one" is a true answer that loses deals
when it arrives without evidence. This is the evidence, plus a real agreement
ready for the day it becomes necessary.

## Part A — What to send an enterprise buyer today

> ARMA Island runs entirely on the user's Mac. It ships without network
> entitlement, so macOS blocks outbound connections at the OS level; it has no
> server, no account system, and no analytics. No customer data is transmitted
> to us, therefore we act as a processor for none of it and a DPA has no
> subject matter.
>
> The only personal data we hold is the licence purchaser's name and email
> address, for which we are an independent controller under our Privacy Policy.
>
> We are glad to confirm the above in writing, to complete a security
> questionnaire, and to sign the attached DPA should our processing ever change.

That paragraph, plus the Privacy Policy, answers most procurement reviews.

## Part B — The agreement, for when it applies

*Sign this only if a future version processes customer data on your behalf —
for example a hosted licence server, a sync service, or team features.*

Between **[CUSTOMER]** ("Controller") and **[TRADER NAME]** ("Processor").

**1. Subject matter and duration.** The Processor processes personal data only
to provide the services in the parties' agreement, for as long as that agreement
lasts.

**2. Nature and purpose.** [DESCRIBE — must match what the software actually
does. Do not sign a generic description.]

**3. Categories of data and data subjects.** [LIST — again, actual.]

**4. Instructions.** The Processor processes personal data only on documented
instructions from the Controller, including on transfers, unless required
otherwise by law — in which case it informs the Controller first unless the law
forbids that.

**5. Confidentiality.** Everyone authorised to process the data is bound by
confidentiality.

**6. Security.** The Processor implements appropriate technical and
organisational measures under Article 32 GDPR, including: encryption in transit
and at rest, access on a least-privilege basis, logging of administrative
access, and documented backup and restore.

**7. Sub-processors.** No sub-processor without the Controller's prior specific
or general written authorisation. Where general, the Processor gives at least 30
days' notice of any change and the Controller may object. Current
sub-processors: [LIST — if none, say "none"].

**8. Data subject rights.** The Processor assists the Controller, by appropriate
technical and organisational measures, in responding to access, rectification,
erasure, restriction, portability and objection requests.

**9. Breach.** The Processor notifies the Controller **without undue delay and
in any event within 48 hours** of becoming aware of a personal data breach, with
the information the Controller needs to meet its own Article 33 obligations.

**10. Assistance.** The Processor assists with data protection impact
assessments and prior consultation, taking into account the nature of processing
and the information available to it.

**11. Deletion or return.** On termination the Processor deletes or returns all
personal data at the Controller's choice, and deletes existing copies, unless
law requires storage.

**12. Audit.** The Processor makes available all information necessary to
demonstrate compliance and allows for and contributes to audits, including
inspections, by the Controller or an auditor it mandates — on reasonable notice
and no more than once a year absent a breach.

**13. Transfers.** Any transfer outside the EEA/UK is made under an adequacy
decision or the Standard Contractual Clauses, which are incorporated by
reference where they apply.

**14. Liability.** As set out in the parties' main agreement.

*Signatures, dates, and an Annex I/II describing processing and security
measures in the form the Controller requires.*
